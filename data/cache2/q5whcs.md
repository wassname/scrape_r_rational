>> [Eating Animals](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/6604712-eating-animals) completely changed my perspective on vegetarianism 10+ years ago. Unfortunately it wasn't enough to actually make me a vegetarian, only a self-aware hypocrite. ^I^do^eat^less^meat^though.
>> [Demian by Herman Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demian) was the book that woke me up to the value of literature as a teenager. It was an intensely intimate experience to the point that I can't even evaluate its value accurately and so can't really recommend it someone else, so keep that in mind. For me it changed how I viewed and consumed fiction, and it disposed me to occasionally reading novels that were out of my comfort/interest range, where a book's value had to be greater than "am I continually entertained?". The mental equivalent of increasing the weights when working out, perhaps. So while I don't necessarily recommend Demian, I do recommend finding an inroad to literary fiction that is palatable to you as an individual.
>> [Das Kapital by Marx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Das_Kapital,_Volume_I). Whatever your beliefs, this book is one of several that is essential reading for anyone who wishes to (begin to) understand how our world works and why it works that way. As with many "foundational books", its utility rests not on whether its arguments are strictly true or false, but on the fact that so many people lived and died believing its ideas to be true(or false), and the effect this has on our current reality. That said, the biggest reason to read this book is that it's really fucking embarrassing to be one of those people who argue so confidently for/against socialism/communism and have only read The Communist Manifesto(if that), which is a pamphlet written to incite illiterate peasants in the 1800's. Don't be that person.
>>
