>>> >__Purim__ (/ˈpʊərɪm/; [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language):  פּוּרִים (help·info) *Pûrîm* "[lots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sortition)", from the word פור *pur*,  related to [Akkadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_language): *pūru*) is a [Jewish holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_holidays#Purim.E2.80.94Festival_of_Lots) that commemorates the deliverance of the [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews) people in the ancient [Persian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) where a plot had been formed to destroy them. The story is recorded in the [Biblical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketuvim) [Book of Esther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Esther) (*Megillat Ester* מגילת אסתר in [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language)).
>>> 
>>> >According to the Book of Esther, [Haman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haman_(Bible\)), royal [vizier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vizier) to King [Ahasuerus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahasuerus) (presumed to be [Xerxes I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xerxes_I) of [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia)   ), planned to kill all the Jews in the empire, but his plans were foiled by [Mordecai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordecai) and his cousin and adopted daughter [Esther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esther), who had risen to become Queen of Persia. The day of deliverance became a day of feasting and rejoicing.
>>> 
>>> >Based on the conclusions of the Scroll of Esther ([Esther 9:22](http://tools.wmflabs.org/bibleversefinder/?book=Esther&verse=9:22&src=HE)): "[...] that they should make them days of feasting and gladness, and of sending portions one to another, and gifts to the poor." Purim is therefore celebrated among Jews by:
>>> 
>>> >
>>> 
>>> >* Exchanging reciprocal gifts of food and drink known as *[mishloach manot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mishloach_manot)*
>>> 
>>> >* Donating [charity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tzedakah) to the poor known as *mattanot la-evyonim* 
>>> 
>>> >* Eating a celebratory meal known as a *[se'udat Purim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seudat_mitzvah#Seudat_Purim)*
>>> 
>>> >* Public recitation ("reading of the megillah") of the Scroll of Esther, known as *kriat ha-megillah*, usually in [synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synagogue)
>>> 
>>> >* Reciting additions, known as *[Al HaNissim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_HaNissim)*, to the daily [prayers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amidah) and the [grace after meals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkat_Hamazon)
>>> 
>>> >Other customs include drinking wine or any other alcoholic beverage, wearing of masks and costumes, and public celebration. 
>>> 
>>> >Purim is celebrated annually according to the [Hebrew calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_calendar) on the 14th day of the [Hebrew month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_calendar#Months) of [Adar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adar) (and on Adar II in Hebrew leap years that take place every 2 to 3 years), the day following the victory of the Jews over their enemies. In cities that were protected by a surrounding wall at the time of the Biblical [Joshua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua), Purim is instead celebrated on the 15th of the month of Adar on what is known as *[Shushan Purim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purim#Shushan_Purim)*, since fighting in the walled city of Shushan continued through the 14th day of Adar.  Today, only [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem) and a few other cities celebrate Purim on the 15th of Adar.
