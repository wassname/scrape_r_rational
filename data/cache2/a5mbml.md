>>>> **u/swirlprism** [+1]  (5 years later)
>>>> 
>>>> I know this comment is 5 years old but what is the ripple effect?

>>>>> **u/alexanderwales** [+1]  *Time flies like an arrow* (5 years later)
>>>>> 
>>>>> Ripple effect is a model of time travel where, when you make a change in the past, it takes some amount of "meta-time" to ripple forward and actually affect the future. This is the model used most famously in *Back to the Future*, where Marty has a limited amount of time to rectify the future or he'll fade away.
>>>>> 
>>>>> It's one of the hardest ones to get firm from a "scifi rules" standpoint, but *narratively* it's easy to understand, because there's a ticking clock and consequences and whatever.

>>>>>> **u/swirlprism** [+1]  (5 years later)
>>>>>> 
>>>>>> How do you reify it and make it follow hard rules?

>>>>>>> **u/alexanderwales** [+2]  *Time flies like an arrow* (5 years later)
>>>>>>> 
>>>>>>> You just need to actually put hard rules in place. You say that meta-time travels at e.g. 1 year per hour, that there are things that *don't* need to ripple, etc.
>>>>>>> 
>>>>>>> You establish a rule that people can "see" ripples approaching, map out when a ripple is going to hit, and *maybe* some people are uniquely immune to the ripple effect, that if there's a version of them in the rippled timeline they just merge, or there's some technology that allows you to make a box that's ripple-immune or whatever.
>>>>>>> 
>>>>>>> The *interesting* thing about ripple effect, as opposed to branching timelines, is that there's a singular timeline to fight over, and if you set it up right, windows of opportunity for action, and a ticking clock that time travel stories don't often get.
>>>>>>> 
>>>>>>> But it's also conceptually difficult, when you get out of the narrative mindset and into the hard scifi mindset.
